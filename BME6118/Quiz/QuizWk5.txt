Nuclear Imaging Part II: Applications Quiz
Q1. Which tracer reports active bone remodeling?
A. 99mTc-MDP
B. 18F-FDG
C. 68Ga-PSMA-11
D. 123I-flutemetamol

Correct answer: A
Explanation: 99mTc-MDP is a bisphosphonate tracer that binds hydroxyapatite during active bone remodeling.

Q2. What does SPECT/CT add to bone imaging?
A. Measures blood glucose
B. Localizes uptake anatomically
C. Measures lung ventilation
D. Detects amyloid plaques

Correct answer: B
Explanation: SPECT/CT combines radiotracer uptake with anatomical localization in the skeleton.

Q3. A stress perfusion defect improves at rest. What does this suggest?
A. Permanent scar
B. Normal perfusion
C. Reversible ischemia
D. Lung embolism

Correct answer: C
Explanation: A stress defect that improves at rest supports reversible myocardial ischemia.

Q4. Which V/Q pattern suggests pulmonary embolism?
A. Normal ventilation, normal perfusion
B. Reduced ventilation, reduced perfusion
C. Increased ventilation, increased perfusion
D. Preserved ventilation, reduced perfusion

Correct answer: D
Explanation: Pulmonary embolism can cause regional perfusion loss while ventilation remains preserved.

Q5. Which tracer is used for lung ventilation imaging?
A. 99mTc aerosol
B. 99mTc-MDP
C. 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose
D. 68Ga-DOTATATE

Correct answer: A
Explanation: Ventilation studies use 99mTc aerosol, such as Technegas, or 81mKr gas.

Q6. What does “ictal” SPECT describe?
A. Imaging between seizures
B. Imaging during a seizure
C. Imaging after chemotherapy
D. Imaging during sleep

Correct answer: B
Explanation: Ictal means during a seizure; involved brain regions usually show increased blood flow.

Q7. Which statement about 123I iodide is correct?
A. It binds PSMA
B. It remains outside thyroid cells
C. It enters through the sodium-iodide symporter
D. It measures bone turnover

Correct answer: C
Explanation: 123I iodide enters thyroid cells through the sodium-iodide symporter and contributes to hormone production.

Q8. Which renal tracer mainly undergoes tubular secretion?
A. 99mTc-DTPA
B. 99mTc-DMSA
C. 18F-FDG
D. 99mTc-MAG3

Correct answer: D
Explanation: 99mTc-MAG3 is mainly handled by tubular secretion and assesses drainage and relative function.

Q9. Why does 18F-FDG accumulate in many cells?
A. It becomes trapped after phosphorylation
B. It binds hydroxyapatite
C. It targets PSMA
D. It enters pulmonary vessels

Correct answer: A
Explanation: Hexokinase converts FDG into FDG-6-phosphate, which accumulates because further glycolysis is limited.

Q10. Which normal tissue usually shows high 18F-FDG uptake?
A. Cortical bone
B. Brain cortex
C. Thyroid cartilage
D. Lung pleura

Correct answer: B
Explanation: The brain cortex normally uses substantial glucose and therefore commonly shows high FDG uptake.

Q11. What does SUV represent in FDG PET?
A. SPECT uptake volume
B. Standard ventilation unit
C. Standardized uptake value
D. Scintillator uniformity value

Correct answer: C
Explanation: SUV is the standardized uptake value used to quantify radiotracer uptake.

Q12. Which condition can cause false-positive FDG uptake?
A. Low glucose use
B. Reduced blood flow
C. Normal bone turnover
D. Infection or recent surgery

Correct answer: D
Explanation: Infection and recent surgery can produce intense FDG uptake and mimic active malignancy.

Q13. What is PSMA commonly overexpressed in?
A. Prostate cancer
B. Thyroiditis
C. Pulmonary embolism
D. Alzheimer’s disease

Correct answer: A
Explanation: PSMA is overexpressed in many prostate cancers and provides a target for PET imaging.

Q14. Why does the PSMA-11 targeting motif remain attached to PSMA?
A. It is radioactive
B. It cannot be cleaved
C. It contains glucose
D. It binds hydroxyapatite

Correct answer: B
Explanation: The Glu-urea-Lys motif mimics a substrate but cannot be cleaved by the PSMA enzyme.

Q15. Which location can show normal PSMA-11 uptake?
A. Cerebral ventricles
B. Pulmonary alveoli
C. Salivary glands
D. Spinal discs

Correct answer: C
Explanation: Normal PSMA-11 uptake occurs in salivary glands, kidneys, and bowel.

Q16. Which receptor is targeted by 68Ga-DOTATATE?
A. Dopamine transporter
B. PSMA
C. Sodium-iodide symporter
D. Somatostatin receptor 2

Correct answer: D
Explanation: DOTATATE targets SSTR2, which is highly expressed on many neuroendocrine tumors.

Q17. What may high FDG uptake and low SSTR uptake indicate?
A. A possible aggressive component
B. A normal receptor-rich phenotype
C. A completely benign lesion
D. A reversible cardiac defect

Correct answer: A
Explanation: Low receptor expression with high glycolytic activity may indicate a more aggressive tumor component.

Q18. Which FDG brain pattern can support Alzheimer’s disease?
A. Isolated cerebellar uptake
B. Temporoparietal hypometabolism
C. Increased frontal metabolism
D. Uniform cortical hypermetabolism

Correct answer: B
Explanation: Temporoparietal metabolic reduction can support an Alzheimer’s disease pattern.

Q19. What does a positive amyloid PET scan generally show?
A. Increased lung ventilation
B. Reduced bone remodeling
C. Reduced gray-white contrast
D. Increased thyroid hormone production

Correct answer: C
Explanation: Amyloid plaque retention increases cortical uptake and reduces gray-white matter contrast.

Q20. What limitation applies to a positive amyloid PET scan?
A. It cannot image the brain
B. It cannot detect cortical uptake
C. It proves active infection
D. It does not establish symptom cause

Correct answer: D
Explanation: Amyloid plaques can occur in cognitively normal older adults, so diagnosis requires clinical context and other findings.
