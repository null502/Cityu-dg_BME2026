Radiology part II: CT Quiz
Q1. What does CT add to conventional radiography?
A. Single projection only
B. Many-angle projections reconstruct 3D attenuation
C. No need for detectors
D. Only bone images
Correct answer: B
Explanation: CT reconstructs attenuation in each voxel from many angular projections.

Q2. A CT detector reading measures what along one ray?
A. Attenuation at one point
B. Photon energy spectrum
C. Total attenuation line integral
D. Detector blur only
Correct answer: C
Explanation: Each reading is a line integral of attenuation along the X-ray path.

Q3. Why does CT need projections from many angles?
A. To reduce patient dose
B. To remove all noise
C. To increase tube voltage
D. To recover missing spatial information
Correct answer: D
Explanation: Angular diversity provides spatial information lost in single line integrals.

Q4. Which equation gives total attenuation p?
A. p = −ln(I/I0)
B. p = I/I0
C. p = I0/I
D. p = e^(−I/I0)
Correct answer: A
Explanation: p equals negative natural log of detected over unattenuated intensity.

Q5. Which material has negative Hounsfield units?
A. Water
B. Fat
C. Cortical bone
D. Iodinated contrast
Correct answer: B
Explanation: Fat is negative HU; water is 0 and bone is positive.

Q6. What does a narrow CT window improve?
A. Radiation dose
B. Z-axis resolution
C. Soft-tissue contrast
D. Metal artifact removal
Correct answer: C
Explanation: Narrow window width improves contrast among similar soft tissues.

Q7. Why filter the sinogram before backprojection?
A. Reduce blurring
B. Increase pitch
C. Lower Hounsfield units
D. Remove patient motion
Correct answer: A
Explanation: Filtered backprojection applies a sharpening filter to reduce blurring.

Q8. Too few angular views in CT produce?
A. Beam hardening
B. Partial volume
C. Photon counting
D. Peripheral streaks
Correct answer: D
Explanation: Too few views produce streaks; too few detector samples cause aliasing.

Q9. Helical CT combines continuous gantry rotation with?
A. Stationary table
B. Continuous table motion
C. Step-and-shoot motion
D. No detector motion
Correct answer: B
Explanation: Source traces a helix as table moves continuously.

Q10. What does higher helical pitch generally do?
A. Improves longitudinal sampling
B. Increases photons per slice
C. Covers anatomy faster
D. Removes motion artifacts
Correct answer: C
Explanation: Higher pitch covers anatomy faster but may reduce longitudinal sampling.

Q11. Multi-slice CT uses multiple detector rows to acquire?
A. One slice per rotation
B. No slice images
C. Only cone-beam dental images
D. Several slices per rotation
Correct answer: D
Explanation: Multiple rows acquire several slices per rotation, shortening exams.

Q12. Cone-beam CT commonly uses what geometry?
A. 3D cone beam and 2D detector
B. Parallel beam only
C. Single detector row
D. No reconstruction
Correct answer: A
Explanation: Cone-beam CT captures a larger volume per rotation; common in dental imaging.

Q13. In an energy-integrating detector, photodiode converts light into?
A. X-rays
B. Heat
C. Electrical charge
D. Photons
Correct answer: C
Explanation: Scintillator converts X-rays to light; photodiode converts light to charge.

Q14. Photon-counting CT uses energy thresholds to?
A. Blur the sinogram
B. Increase pitch
C. Window images only
D. Sort photons into bins
Correct answer: D
Explanation: Energy thresholds sort individual photons into bins for spectral information.

Q15. Why use diastolic phase for cardiac CT?
A. Heart is less mobile
B. Contrast is absent
C. Bone is denser
D. Dose is zero
Correct answer: A
Explanation: Diastolic phase reduces coronary motion blur.

Q16. Perfusion CT follows what through tissue over time?
A. Air bolus
B. Iodinated contrast
C. FDG
D. Bone mineral
Correct answer: B
Explanation: Repeated images track iodinated contrast; time-attenuation curves are analyzed.

Q17. Dual-energy CT can separate which pair with similar HU?
A. Air and water
B. Muscle and fat
C. Metal and air
D. Iodine and bone
Correct answer: D
Explanation: Iodine, calcium, and water have distinct spectral signatures at two energies.

Q18. Beam hardening causes which CT artifact?
A. Stair-step artifact
B. Motion blur
C. Cupping and dark bands
D. Partial volume
Correct answer: C
Explanation: Low-energy photons are absorbed preferentially, producing cupping and dark bands.

Q19. What does a CT voxel report in partial-volume effect?
A. Average attenuation of materials inside
B. Only bone attenuation
C. Only air attenuation
D. Exact photon count
Correct answer: A
Explanation: Voxel averages all materials; small structures may disappear or have biased HU.

Q20. Non-contrast head CT rapidly detects which condition?
A. Amyloid plaques
B. Acute hemorrhage
C. Lung nodules
D. Prostate cancer
Correct answer: B
Explanation: Non-contrast CT rapidly detects acute blood and mass effect in trauma.
