\section{1. Introduction \& Integration}
\subsection{What is flexible bioelectronics}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \key{Electronics:} physics, engineering, technology and applications
  dealing with the emission, flow and control of electrons in vacuum and matter.
  \item \key{Flexible electronics:} the production of electronic components fabricated on a
  flexible substrate (typically polymer), giving thinner, lighter, softer,
  conformal and potentially stretchable systems.
  \item Main course goal: integrate high-performance electronics with soft,
  dynamic and biologically compatible interfaces for on-patient monitoring,
  diagnosis, therapy and human--machine interaction.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Electronic categories}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Digital, analogue, microelectronics, integrated circuits (ICs),
  power electronics, optoelectronics, semiconductor devices and embedded systems.
  \item \key{Discrete circuit:} individually packaged components; customizable
  but bulky and costly at high volume.
  \item \key{Monolithic circuit:} devices fabricated on one substrate; high
  density, low unit cost at volume, but requires cleanroom processing and is
  fixed after design.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Integration and Moore's law}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Integration evolved from vacuum tubes to transistors, ICs and
  nanoscale CMOS; resistor, capacitor, diode and transistor can be made in
  compatible semiconductor processes.
  \includegraphics[width=1\linewidth]{BasicComponents.png}
  \item \key{Moore's law:} transistor count in a dense IC approximately
  doubles every two years (an observation/trend, not a physical law).
  \item Scaling improves switching speed, energy use and miniaturization,
  but introduces purity, heat, fabrication and mass-production challenges.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why flexible bioelectronics}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \key{Manufacturing cost:} Modern intel fabrication capital cost > 3,000,000,000.00 USD, 
  while modest flex electronics fabrication capital cost < 3,000,000.00 USD
  \item \key{Market:} enables embedding simple intelligence with cost-efficient manufacturing techniques 
  \item Conventional devices are hard/planar; organs and skin are soft,
  curved, moving and hydrated. Rigid interfaces cause discomfort, poor
  contact, motion artefact and low-fidelity signals.
  \item \key{Healthcare challenges:} centralized, slow, invasive, expensive,
  expertise-dependent testing and delayed diagnosis.
  \item \key{Desired properties:} conformality, flexibility/stretchability,
  stable adhesion, breathability, biocompatibility, low power and wireless
  communication.
  \item \key{Key design principle:} preserve high-performance active materials
  while reducing effective stiffness by thinning, patterning or embedding
  them in soft substrates.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Cross of biology and devices}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Biology | Interface | Device
  \item Interface needs: High performance, flexible, high though-put
\end{itemize}

\subsection{E.g. Bio-Compatible Soft ECoG}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \key{Currently:} with wires, monitored by bulky medical equipment
  \item \key{Future:} wireless, monitored by portable interfaces, such as smartphones
\end{itemize}

\subsection{E.g. Complex 3D Structures manufacturing}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \key{Method:} Stress-Induced Folding, Two-Photon Lithography, 
  Direct-Writing Printing, 3D Photopatterning
  \item Can we exploit today’s the most advanced manufacturing technologies in semiconductor industry? \\
  -Yes, but selectively. Today’s advanced semiconductor manufacturing—EUV lithography, 
  atomic-layer deposition, nanoscale etching, and heterogeneous integration—can be exploited, 
  but access is limited by cost, equipment, materials, yield, and specialized expertise.
  \item Can we nano/microfab electronics from the existing highest-performance electronic materials 
  and then transform them into 3D devices?\\
  -Yes, in principle. High-performance materials can be nano/microfabricated and integrated into 3D devices using stacking, 
  transfer printing, folding, origami/kirigami, molding, and additive fabrication. 
  However, preserving electrical performance during deformation, ensuring reliable interconnects, heat dissipation, 
  and scalable manufacturing remain major challenges.
\end{itemize}


\section{2. Devices \& Materials}
\subsection{Band theory}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \key{Electrical conductivity:} a measure of the number of charge carriers available for electric-field acceleration.
  \item Nature of the band picture of each solid can be indicative of conductivity
  \includegraphics[width=1\linewidth]{BandTheory.png}
  \item Atomic orbitals broaden into energy bands: valence band (VB),
  conduction band (CB), separated by band gap $E_g$.
  \item \key{Fermi level}: energy with maximum electron occupation
  probability at absolute zero.
  \item Metals: overlapping bands or $E_g\approx0$; semiconductors: moderate
  gap; insulators: large gap. \\
  E.g. Si $E_g=1.12$ eV, Ge $=0.66$ eV, GaAs $=1.41$ eV, SiO$_2\approx9$ eV, diamond $=5.47$ eV.
  \item Donors (N, P, As) produce \key{n-type}; acceptors (B, Al, Ga)
  produce \key{p-type} material.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Electronic building blocks}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \key{Resistor/capacitor:} a broad range of applications, from electronics circuit to sensors, to actuators. \\
  Pressure, strain, temperature, gas sensors and photodetectors.\\
  E.g. Strain gauges, Thermal couples, Gas detectors.
  \item \key{Diode:} Well-known as rectifiers. Widely used in IC. \\
  Emission color is set by semiconductor band gap.\\
  \includegraphics[width=1\linewidth]{LEDColor.png}
  E.g. Photodetectors, Solar cells, Emitting components, LED. \\
  \item \key{Transistor:} Signal amplification for biological signals.\\
  E.g. Electrical switches, Amplifier, Gas/Tactile sensors and Ion-to-electron converter.\\
  Important for amplifying weak biological signals.\\
  Transiting ionic shifts in biological fluids into readable electrical currents.\\
  \includegraphics[width=1\linewidth]{TransistorConsist.png}
  Linear Mobility ($\mu_{\rm lin}$) and Saturation Mobility ($\mu_{\rm sat}$)：
\end{itemize}

\formula{\displaystyle

\subsection{Crystal and material classes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \key{Common crystal structure:} 
  Face-centered cubic (FCC), body-centered cubic (BCC) and hexagonal close-packed (HCP).
  \item Si has diamond lattice; Miller notation: $[abc]$ direction, $(abc)$ plane; 
  crystal orientation affects wafer processing and anisotropic etching.
  \item \key{Crystal structure:} \\
  Single crystal, long-range order and high electronic performance.\\
  Polycrystal, grain boundaries affect transport. \\
  Amorphous materials have lower thermal conductivity, can be transparent and stable, but deform more.\\
  \item Candidate materials: Si, III--V (GaAs), metal oxides, polymers,
  small molecules, graphene and carbon nanotubes.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Soft and emerging materials}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Conventional electronics fail at only about $1$--$3\%$ strain;
  skin reaches $\sim30\%$ and joints can reach $\sim100\%$.
  \item \key{Flexible electronics advantages:}\\
  \key{Soft:} like human skin, it provides inspiration by its ability to sense 
  (touch, temperature, humidity), self-heal, and regulate temperature.\\
  \key{Mechanical properties:}Electronic materials that are intrinsically stretchable 
  have mechanical properties defined by the encasing material.\\
  \key{Body compatibility:} Materials that are softer than skin can make comfortable, 
  conformal, and 'mechanically transparent' interfaces with the body for 
  'electronic skin' (e-skin) and prosthesis.\\
  \key{Adaptability:} soft electronics integrate well with soft robotics.
  \item \key{Liquid metals:} a class of soft materials that can flow to enable 
  'shape reconfigurable' electronic components and self healing structures.\\
  Liquid metals are intrinsically deformable, with Highly conductive, 
  which maintains metallic conductivity up to ~700 \% strain.\\
  E.g. Ga, eutectic gallium indium (EGaIn, 75.2\% Ga, 24.8\% In) and Galinstan
  (67 \% Ga, 20.5 \% In and 12.5 \% Sn).\\
  Ga alloys form an oxide skin in oxygen; injection/vacuum filling patterns
  them into microchannels.
  \item \key{2D materials:} graphene, black phosphorus and MXene.\\
  Single/few atomic layers give high surface area, conductivity,
  transparency, flexibility and easy functionalization.\\
  \item \key{Some other materials:} liquid crystal(like liquid crystal display, LCD),
  organic materials(like organic light-emitting diode, OLED), 
  polymers(like amorphous polymers, PMMA; semicrystalline polymers, 
  PP, PE, PET; rystalline polymers, PTFE; polyvinylidene fluoride, PVDF),
  elastomers(like silicone elastomer, PDMS), woven fabrics.
\end{itemize}


\section{3. Mechanics \& Design}
\subsection{Epidermal sensors}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \key{Material choose:} soft polymer (PDMS, Polyurethan, hydrogels etc.)
  and composite as substrate; match human tissues, minimizing mechanical
  mismatch and discomfort; matching Young's modulus of a device materials to that of 
  biological organs or tissue is critical for stretchable bioelectronics.
  \item \key{Why epidermal sensors} High-fidelity is the key to the success 
  of wearable health monitors, which essentially demands both high signal-to-noise 
  ratio and low susceptibility to motion artifacts.State-of-the-art wearable devices 
  such as the wrist or arm bands and chest straps are not capable of intimate-skin 
  integration and hence cannot provide medical-grade data.\\
  Pattern intrinsically stiff and blanket high-performance nanomembranes into 
  structurally soft and stretchable filamentary serpentines, and transfer print them 
  from rigid wafers to ultrathin, skin-like elastomeric substrates could solve this problem.
  \item \key{Essential properties:}\\
  Conformality: sensors must closely match and conform to the topography of 
  human skin to maintain comfort and accurate physiological monitoring.\\
  Flexibility and Stretchability: To accommodate natural motion and skin 
  deformation, sensor require stretchability.\\
  Stable adhesion: adhesive layers or structural microfeatures are 
  needed to keep sensors securely.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Conformability}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Conformality is the ability to maintain intimate contact with
  rough, curved skin. Thickness dominates: thinner layers bend more easily
  and follow topography better.
\end{itemize}
\formula{\displaystyle
D=\frac{Et^3}{12(1-\nu^2)}}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $D$: bending stiffness; $E$: Young's modulus; $t$: thickness;
  $\nu$: Poisson ratio. The cubic $t^3$ dependence means a small thickness
  reduction greatly lowers stiffness.
  \item Engineer low $E$ and low $t$, while retaining electrical and
  mechanical reliability.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Breathability}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Permeable structures allow moisture and air exchanges, 
  reducing sweat accumulation and preventing skin issue during prolonged use.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Biodegradability}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Material should not cause biotoxicity, irritation, allergic reactions, 
  or disrupt normal skin function, even during long term wear.
  \item The use of biodegradable materials in electronics can reduce the 
  accumulation of persistent solid waste. Biodegradable materials allow devices 
  to safely dissolve in the body, eliminating the need for a second surgery and 
  reducing medical risks, cost, and patient discomfort.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Stretchable layouts}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \key{Wavy/wrinkled}: prestrain a soft substrate, attach a stiff thin
  film, then release; out-of-plane waves accommodate extension.
  \item \key{Serpentine}: meandering ribbons rotate, bend and buckle so
  material strain is much lower than applied strain. \\
  The end-to-end distance of a unit cell is denoted by S, which is a function of:
  width $w$, arc radius $R$, arc angle $\alpha$, arm length $l$. \\
  The effective applied strain εapp is defined as:\\
  \formula{\displaystyle \epsilon_{app} = \frac{2 u_0}{S}}
  Smaller $w/R$ and longer arms generally improve stretchability.
  \item \key{Island-bridge}: rigid functional islands connected by soft,
  wavy or serpentine bridges. Non-coplanar layouts are usually more
  stretchable; fractal interconnects increase coverage and compliance.
  \item Origami/kirigami and composite structures add geometric
  deformation, fatigue resistance and mechanical buffering.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Interface requirements}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \key{Adhesion}: must be strong, conformal and durable under motion,
  sweat and repeated loading. Dry adhesion uses van der Waals, electrostatic,
  friction or mechanical interlocking; wet adhesion uses capillary,
  hydrogen-bonding, viscous and chemical interactions.
  \item \key{Breathability}: permits air/moisture exchange and reduces sweat
  accumulation. \key{Biocompatibility}: no toxicity, irritation, allergy or
  disruption of tissue function. \key{Biodegradability}: safe dissolution
  can avoid retrieval surgery and persistent waste.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Biomarker classes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \key{Electrolytes:} Na$^+$, K$^+$, Mg$^{2+}$, Ca$^{2+}$, Cl$^-$ etc.;\\
  changes in specific condition like hydration, cardiac, liver and metabolic status.
  \item \key{Metabolites:} glucose, lactate, uric acid, alcohol and amino acids; 
  biomarkers for metabolic diseases, diabetes and physical performance; 
  sweat and ISF glucose can correalte with blood glucose, but the signal is really small.\\
  E.g. sweat glucose is typically 10--100 times lower than blood glucose.\\
  Lactate is a unique biomarker. 
  Sweat lactate is useful for monitoring sweat gland activity and thermoregulation; 
  Blood lactate is key for assessing systematic metabolic and clinical conditions.
  \item \key{Hormones:} chemical messenger of the endocrine system, affecting metabolism, 
  growth, sexual function, reproduction, sleep and mood, tissue function.\\
  Cortisol, estradiol and other peptide hormones.\\
  Measure for stress, reproductive health and endocrine disorders.
  \item \key{Proteins/enzymes:} active in biological processes like enzymatic reactions, 
  substance and energy transport, and cytoskeleton construction.\\
  Inflammatory marker, cardiac troponins, lactate dehydrogenase, tyrosinase and others.\\
  Cytokine and neuropeptides are important class of protein and peptide biomarker.\\
  Proinflammatory cytokines: IL-1$\alpha$, IL-1$\beta$, IL-6, TNF-$\alpha$, 
  IL-8 are commonly detected in sweat.\\
  Neuropeptide: Neuropeptide Y(NPY), substance P, calcitonin gene-related peptide 
  (CGRP) play roles in mood, pain, satiety, and stress regulation.
  \item \key{Others:} nucleic acids; bacteria/cells; 
  antibiotics or other medication presence support therapeutic drug monitoring;
  small molecules like nutrients, vitamins, and signaling small molecules, 
  emerging biomarkers for personalize health and nutrient monitoring.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Recognition and transduction}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \key{How to sense biomarkers:}\\
  Biorecognition elements are core molecular components in biosensors.\\
  Specifically bind to target analytes, enabling selective detection.\\
  Convert presence of biomolecules into a measurable signals.\\
  Immobilized on solid-state transducer surface.
  \item Biorecognition elements: enzymes, antibodies/affinity proteins,
  peptides, aptamers, receptors and molecularly imprinted polymers (MIPs).
  They bind/catalyse the target and are immobilized on a transducer.
  \item Enzyme example: glucose oxidase catalyses glucose, producing H$_2$O$_2$;

  \item Aptamer: synthetic DNA/RNA sequences bind target; target-induced
  conformational change changes current, voltage, impedance, fluorescence
  or color. \\
  Modes include sandwich or sandwich-like mode, target-induced dissociation(TID), 
  competitive replacement and structure switching.

\subsection{Wafer cleaning}
\begin{itemize}
  \item DI-water ultrasonic/megasonic clean removes particles and soluble ions.
  \item RCA SC-1: DI:H$_4$NOH:H$_2$O$_2=5:1:1$, H$_4$NOH is ammonia water, 29\% by weight of NH3; 
  H$_2$O$_2$ is 30\% hydrogen peroxide. Typically $75$--$80^\circ$C for 10 min; 
  organic/particle clean, forms $\sim1$ nm SiO$_2$.
  \item Optional HF 1:50--1:100, about 15 s at room temperature, strips oxide.
  \item RCA SC-2: DI:HCl:H$_2$O$_2=6:1:1$, $75$--$80^\circ$C for 10 min;
  removes ionic/metal contaminants and passivates the surface.
\end{itemize}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \key{Alternatives}: \\
  Wet methods: piranha ($\mathrm{H_2SO_4:H_2O_2=3:1}$, 10-30 min, extremely exothermic, self heating up to $80^\circ$C); 
  ozone($\mathrm{O_3}$); Organic solvent (Acetone/Isopropanol/DI water)\\
  Dry methods: UV-ozone and plasma cleaning.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Photolithography}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \key{Why use yellow light:}Yellow light (wavelength of about 590-620 nm) can not only effectively reduce 
  ultraviolet (UV) radiation from the light source, but also enhance the lighting effect of visible light, 
  thereby reducing damage to sensitive products. Clean rooms using yellow light can reduce energy consumption by 15\%-30\%.
  \item \key{Typical flow:} substrate clean $\rightarrow$ photoresist coat/spin
  $\rightarrow$ soft bake $\rightarrow$ mask align/expose $\rightarrow$
  develop $\rightarrow$ hard bake $\rightarrow$ etch/deposit/lift-off.
  \item \key{Photoresists:} positive resist exposed region dissolves in developer; negative
  resist: exposed region cross-links and remains.
  \item \key{Photomask:} made from Film or very flat pieces of quartz with a layer of Chromium (Cr) on one side.
  Alignment marks and backside/IR alignment enable multilayer MEMS processing.
  \item Phase-shift mask changes adjacent phase by $180^\circ$ for destructive interference; 
  optical proximity correction pre-distorts the mask.
  \item  Alignment marks printed on the wafer could be eroded after layers of processes. 
  This could be resolved by introducing new markers in later layers.
  \item \key{Light Interact With Resist:} There is a minimum total optical energy (dose, in mJ/area) 
  needed to fully expose the photoresist (Energy Integrator).\\
  $D_0$ = Lowest energy (dose) needed to “drive the photochemistry”\\
  $D_{100}$ = Lowest energy for which all resist is removed.\\
  Measured by characterizing the post-exposure resist thickness as a function of exposure dose.
  \item Lithography resolution can be dramatically improved by using optical sources with smaller wavelengths
  \includegraphics[width=1\linewidth]{Wavelength.png}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Other patterning}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \key{E-beam lithography}: direct-write, high resolution, slow and serial; useful for research and masks.
  \item \key{Soft lithography}: elastomeric stamp/mold; low cost and compatible with large-area flexible substrates.
  \item \key{Laser patterning:}\\
  Laser direct writing (LDW): Laser to write patterns directly onto a substrate, creating conductive features.\\
  Laser engraving: Laser remove material to carve out conductive structure (LM channel embedded in substrate).\\
  Laser scribing (LSG) or Laser-induced graphene (LIG): Laser converts polymer precursors to conductive graphene.
\end{itemize}


\section{6. Processing II: Deposition \& Etching}
\subsection{Vacuum and PVD}
\begin{itemize}
  \item PVD: Physical Vapor Deposition; \\ CVD: Chemical Vapor Deposition.
  \item $1~\mathrm{atm}=760~\mathrm{torr}=1.013\times10^5$ Pa;
  $1~\mathrm{torr}=133.3$ Pa. Vacuum reduces collisions and contamination.
  Base pressure is reached before process gas; sputtering then introduces
  Ar at working pressure (typically millitorr range) to sustain plasma.
  \item \key{Mass transport:} $\lambda \approx \frac{0.5}{pressure(Pa)}$
  \item \key{Evaporation}: source is heated (thermal/e-beam); atoms travel
  ballistically to substrate, giving line-of-sight coverage.
  \item \key{Evaporation rate:} 
  \formula{R_{evap}=5.83 \times 10^{-2}A_s \sqrt{\frac{m}{T}} P_e} \\
  inside $R_{evap}$ is Evaporation rate (g/s); $A_s$ is area of sources (cm2);
  $m$ is molecular weight (g/mol); $T$ is temperature (K); 
  $P_e$ is vapor pressure of sources (Torr) (not chamber pressure).
  \item \key{Sputtering}: Ar plasma ions bombard target and eject atoms;
  high-energy transport, high deposition rate and better suitability for refractory metals such as Ti/W. 
  Co-sputtering controls composition.
  \item Sputter has advantages like higher pressure than evaporation, high deposition rate, 
  better uniformity and step coverage, better stoichiometry control, and it work for most materials.\\
  It has disadvantages like plasma induced damages(etching), more impure and defects, 
  not good for single crystal epitaxy and mostly polycrystalline and amorphous films.\\
  \includegraphics[width=1\linewidth]{StepCoverage.png}
  \item Interconnect trade-off: Al is cheap, stable, easy to deposit/etch and
  diffuses little in Si/SiO$_2$; Cu is more conductive and reduces RC delay
  below about 130 nm, but requires diffusion barriers/processing control.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{CVD, ALD and solution deposition}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \key{CVD}: gaseous precursors react/decompose at the surface;
  controls include temperature, pressure, gas type/flow and time, 
  determining thickness, crystallinity and film quality.
\end{itemize}
\formula{\displaystyle \mathrm{SiH_4(g)\rightarrow Si(s)+2H_2(g)}}
\begin{itemize}
  \item with condition $T:580-650^\circ C, P:0.1-1  \mathrm{Torr}$
  \item \key{Plasma Enhanced CVD, PECVD:} plasma increases ion energy,

  \key{ALD:} self-limiting, layer-by-layer, highly uniform and gives precise thickness control.

\subsection{Etching}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \key{Etching} removes desired material selectively; \key{corrosion}
  is unwanted attack. Wet etching uses liquid, dry etching uses gas/plasma.
  Important metrics: etch rate, selectivity and anisotropy.
  \item Isotropic etch attacks laterally and vertically; anisotropic etch
  preserves sidewalls and directionality.
  \item Examples: HF etches SiO$_2$; FeCl$_3$ etches Cu; KI/I$_2$ etches Au.
  KOH etches Si anisotropically with approximate rate ratio
  $(111):(110):(100)=1:600:400$ and can use SiO$_2$/Si$_3$N$_4$ masks.
\end{itemize}
